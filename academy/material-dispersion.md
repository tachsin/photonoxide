## 1. Why does a material's index depend on wavelength?

::coming
Bound electrons and lattice vibrations as driven oscillators: the index rises towards each
resonance, and a transparent material sits between its infrared and ultraviolet resonances.

## 2. What do the Sellmeier, Lorentz, Drude and Cauchy models describe?

::coming silica_index
Each model's formula, the physics behind its terms, and the range of wavelengths over which a
fit of it can be trusted.

## 3. Where does fused silica's index come from?

::coming silica_index
Malitson's 1965 measurements and his three-term Sellmeier formula at 20 °C, reproduced from 0.55
to 3.2 µm to the six decimals of his Table I.

## 4. Where does silicon's index come from?

::coming silicon_index
Li's 1980 table at 293 K, its stated uncertainty of ±2 × 10⁻⁴, the natural cubic spline the
catalogue draws through it, and the example's check from 1.3 to 10 µm.

## 5. What do the group index and group-velocity dispersion of a material measure?

::coming
$n_g = n - \lambda\thinspace dn/d\lambda$ and the second derivative that spreads a pulse, both from
the same dispersion model, for silicon and silica near 1550 nm.
