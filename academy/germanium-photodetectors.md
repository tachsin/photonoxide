## 1. Why germanium?

::coming
Silicon is transparent at 1.31 and 1.55 µm; germanium, grown on it, absorbs there, its direct gap
near 0.8 eV, and its strain on silicon stretches its absorption further into the C band.

## 2. How much light does it absorb?

::coming
Germanium's complex index with its provenance, a waveguide's mode coupled into a germanium
region, its absorbed fraction along the detector, and the responsivity in amperes per watt.

## 3. What sets the bandwidth?

::coming
The carriers' transit across the depleted region, the junction's capacitance through its
resistance, and the trade between a short, fast detector and a long, absorbing one.

## 4. What is dark current?

::coming
The current without light, from defects at the germanium–silicon interface, and why it sets the
weakest signal a receiver can read.

## 5. What do integrated detectors reach today?

::coming
Lateral and vertical junctions, detectors fed by silicon nitride, and bandwidths beyond 100 GHz,
as Wang et al.'s O-band detector shows.
