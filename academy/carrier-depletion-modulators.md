## 1. How do free carriers change silicon's index?

::coming
The plasma dispersion effect: electrons and holes lower the index and absorb, by Soref and
Bennett's and Nedeljkovic et al.'s fits to measured spectra, at 1.31 and 1.55 µm.

## 2. How does a junction deplete under bias?

::coming
A p–n junction across a rib waveguide, its depletion width growing with the reverse voltage, by
drift-diffusion on the cross-section, and the change in carriers the mode sees.

## 3. What sets the efficiency and the loss?

::coming
Vπ·L from the overlap of the mode with the depleted region, the loss the remaining carriers
cause, and the doping that trades one against the other, as Yu et al.'s lateral junction shows.

## 4. How fast can it switch?

::coming
The junction's capacitance and series resistance, a travelling-wave electrode loaded by the
junction, its velocity and impedance matched, as Patel et al.'s modulator reaches 41 GHz.

## 5. How do slow light and rings make it shorter?

::coming
A corrugated slow-light waveguide that multiplies the phase per length, as Han et al.'s 124 µm
modulator reaches 110 GHz, and ring modulators that trade bandwidth for size.

## 6. How is a modulator driven and measured?

::coming
PRBS and PAM-4 signals through the modulator's large-signal model, eye diagrams, extinction ratio
and optical modulation amplitude, as 0.8's time-domain circuits compute them.
