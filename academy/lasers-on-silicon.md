## 1. Why can't silicon make its own light?

::coming
An indirect band gap, where an electron needs a phonon to recombine with a hole and gives up its
energy as heat far more often than as light.

## 2. How is III–V gain put on silicon?

::coming
Lasers attached at the chip's edge, III–V dies bonded to the wafer and processed with it, and the
coupling between the III–V gain and the silicon waveguide below it.

## 3. Can lasers be grown on silicon?

::coming
Quantum-dot III–V lasers grown on silicon despite their mismatched lattices, and lasers embedded
in the silicon-on-insulator wafer, as Wei et al.'s are.

## 4. What sets a laser's linewidth and noise?

::coming
Long, low-loss external cavities of silicon nitride rings and gratings, and III–V gain integrated
in 3D with them, as Xiang et al.'s ultralow-noise lasers show.

## 5. How does one laser give many wavelengths?

::coming
A Kerr comb in a nitride ring pumped by one laser, its lines a link's channels, as the nonlinear
optics lesson computes them.
