A bent waveguide radiates: beyond some radius its mode would have to travel faster than light
in the cladding, so it leaks there. Its effective index, taken along the arc at a reference
radius R, becomes complex, and $\operatorname{Im} n_\text{eff}$ is the bend's radiation loss. photonoxide
computes it two ways.

## The exact bent slab

A slab bent around an axis y carries modes $\psi(\rho)\thinspace e^{i(\nu\varphi - \omega t)}$, with complex angular
order $\nu = k n_\text{eff} R$. In each layer of index n,

$$
\psi'' + \frac{\psi'}{\rho} + \left(k^2 n^2 - \frac{\nu^2}{\rho^2}\right)\psi = 0,
$$

with ψ and ψ′ continuous at interfaces for TE (E along the axis), and ψ and ψ′/n² for TM.
Outside the last layer the field is exactly the outgoing Hankel function $H^{(1)}_\nu(kn\rho)$
(Marcuse's Eq. 10, written there for $e^{+i\omega t}$). Marcuse approximates the field inside by
the straight guide's; `mode::bend` doesn't:

- from inside, the solution growing outwards through the inner cladding is integrated
  (fourth-order Runge–Kutta) to the middle of the stack;
- from outside, beyond the outer turning point, the outgoing wave's $\psi'/\psi$ comes from Debye's
  expansion of $H^{(1)}_\nu$ (DLMF 10.19.6), its polynomials generated by the recursions of DLMF
  10.41.9 and 10.41.11 and checked against the large-argument expansion (DLMF 10.17.5), and is
  integrated inwards to the same point;
- ν is the root of the difference of the two $\psi'/\psi$, by secant iteration; `fundamental`
  follows the mode in from eight times the radius.

The loss resolves down to $\operatorname{Im} n_\text{eff}/\operatorname{Re} n_\text{eff} \approx 10^{-16}$, where the outgoing wave's
share of the field is lost to round-off.

## Any cross-section, by a conformal map

Heiblum and Harris's map $u = R \ln(1 + x/R)$ turns a bend into a straight guide whose permittivity
grows outwards as $e^{2u/R} = (1 + x/R)^2$ (their Eqs. 9–10). `CrossSection::bent` builds it: nodes
uniform in the physical x (so interfaces stay on nodes) mapped to u, each cell's permittivity
scaled. The outside of the bend needs a [PML](pml.md).

The map is exact for the scalar wave equation, so for **E normal to the bend plane**. For **E in
the bend plane** the exact equivalent medium is anisotropic in both ε and μ; scaling the isotropic
ε adds a term whose first-order effect on a bound mode vanishes, so its error falls with the
radius. A silicon strip bent in the chip plane has its TE-like mode in this case.

## Validation

The book's strip as a slab (2.845, 500 nm, in 1.444, 1.55 µm), against the exact bend:

| | R = 1 µm | R = 2 µm |
|---|---|---|
| E normal: Re error at 10, 5, 2.5 nm | −2.3e-4, −5.8e-5, −1.4e-5 (second order) | −1.5e-4, −3.7e-5, −9.4e-6 |
| E normal: loss | within 0.03 % (Im 9.29e-4) | within 0.08 % (Im 5.06e-7) |
| E in the plane: Re error, loss | 1.3e-3, 8 % | 1.9e-4, 4 % |

At R = 3 µm, E in the plane: 1.3e-4 and 1 %. Below $\operatorname{Im} n_\text{eff} \approx 10^{-12}$ the full-vector
eigensolver can't resolve the loss (at R = 5 µm it is $3.8 \times 10^{-17}$).

**Marcuse's formula** (Eqs. 32–33), an approximation for large radii, against the exact loss of a
slab of 1.6 in 1.5, 1 µm thick, at 1 µm: ratios 1.83, 1.36, 1.14, 1.08, 1.06 at R = 20, 40, 80, 120,
160 µm, the difference falling as 1/R. A large radius gives the straight slab back, at 1/R².
